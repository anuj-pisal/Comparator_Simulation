How an Op-Amp Comparator Works

An **operational amplifier (op-amp)** operating without negative feedback has astronomical open-loop gain (typically $A_{OL} \ge 100{,}000$). Because of this immense gain, any minute difference between its input terminals drives the output directly into one of the power supply saturation rails.

Inverting Comparator Rule:
  • Vin < Vref: Output is pulled HIGH ($\approx +V_{CC}$, Logic 1).
  • Vin > Vref: Output is pulled LOW ($\approx 0\text{V}$, Logic 0).

Schmitt Trigger Hysteresis: In real physical systems, electrical noise near $V_{in} = V_{ref}$ can cause rapid, chaotic output flickering (chatter). Adding hysteresis creates two distinct switching thresholds ($V_{upper}$ and $V_{lower}$), preventing noise spikes from creating false transitions.
